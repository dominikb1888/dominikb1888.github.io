The Cognitive Organization

Information has always been hat the heart of the organization. It’s fundamental distinction from markets is based on the cost of information processing. Standardized goods can easily be sold on market, because they yield standardized contracts. Individualized goods are more efficiently produced in a hierachically integrated organization. The costs of creating and securing the necessary contracts (work and property) take time and capital.

Within information systems, communities as a third option have long been discussed. Open Innovation has stretched these clear boundaries of firms that operate as (more or less) hierarchical organizations on markets to the extreme. Independent if you support inside-out or outside-in, structured or community-driven open innovation. More shades of grey have appeared that did not invalidate the core efficient-market hypothesis.

At its core was that the costs of decision-making are high the more individualized or novel a good is. What if AI challenges this hypothesis? AI is a semantic alignment engine. The costs of this semantic alignment are driven down more-and-more.

The question now begs, which decision-making do we need to secure when semantic alignment is not a problem anymore>
