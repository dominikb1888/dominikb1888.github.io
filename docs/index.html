The Cognitive Organisation: Coase and the Cost of AI

Feb 22, 2022

In 1937, a young British economist named Ronald Coase asked a disarmingly simple question: if free markets are the most efficient mechanism for allocating resources, why do business firms exist at all?

His answer founded the economics of organisation. Markets are brilliant, Coase noted, but using them is expensive. Discovering prices, negotiating terms, drawing up bespoke contracts, and inspecting deliverables all incur “transaction costs.” Where those costs are high—particularly for complex, uncertain, or highly customised work—it is cheaper to bring people under one roof and coordinate them through managerial command.

From Scratch: The Case for Peeling the Veneer

Feb 20, 2022

In 2009, a British designer named Thomas Thwaites set out to build a commercial toaster from scratch. Not to assemble one from a kit, but to smelt iron ore in a chimney, refine crude oil into plastic resin, and draw copper wire from scrap metal. Nine months and over a thousand pounds later, he produced a grotesque, melted contraption that functioned for five seconds before destroying its own element.

Thwaites was not trying to become a competitive appliance manufacturer. He was demonstrating a fundamental condition of modernity: human beings now live surrounded by artefacts whose origins they can neither comprehend nor replicate.

Bismarck’s Ghost: The Invention of the Patient

Jul 10, 2021

Language reveals theology. In modern political discourse, we use “health” and “medicine” interchangeably, as if a physician’s consulting room and human vitality were identical concepts. They are not.

Health is a continuous biological state—a dynamic equilibrium between an organism, its genetics, and its environment. Medicine, by contrast, is a professional guild dedicated to crisis intervention: the acute diagnosis and repair of broken biological machinery.

Confusing the two was harmless when our diagnostic tools were primitive and our therapies limited to setting fractures and prescribing antibiotics. In the era of continuous biometrics, genomic profiling, and ambient telemetry, the confusion has become financially and philosophically unsustainable.
